Design system · v1 · 9 Nov 2026

CenterSpot

Draw the move, not just the shape.

An iPhone and iPad app that turns a formation into a step-by-step animated move and exports it as a vertical video, an image or a .tactic file. This page is the system it is built from: the mark, the tokens, the icons, every component and the words.

Built with
Swift 6 and SwiftUI, iOS and iPadOS 26
Renderer
One, for the screen and the export
Theme
Dark only
Source
CenterSpot/Design/Tokens.swift

The pitch is the hero

Chrome stays quiet so the board carries the colour. One lime lead per screen (D-80).

Never colour alone

Us filled, them rings. Runs solid, passes dashed, dribbles wavy (D-15).

What plays is what exports

One renderer and one timeline for the screen, the preview and the file (D-06, D-07).

A coach's voice

Plain football words. Handwriting only for what a coach would write on the board.

1

Logo

The centre circle as the Broadcast camera sees it. The lime dot in the middle is the centre spot: the mark says the name.

Story

Every move starts at the centre spot. The mark is the halfway line and centre circle, tilted the way CenterSpot's Broadcast camera tilts the pitch, with the near side drawn thicker than the far side, as perspective draws it. The spot is lime, the colour of our side.

CenterSpot

Halfway line

Runs edge to edge, like the line on a pitch: the board is split, the move goes forward.

Tilted circle

The Broadcast camera's view, the app's own look. Far side 4 units, near side 9.

Centre spot

The only colour. Lime on dark, lime with an ink keyline on paper, ink cut out of a lime tile.

Construction

Drawn on a 100-unit grid. Two ellipses make the ring: the outer one, and an inner cut raised 2.5 units so the far side thins to 4 and the near side thickens to 9.

Sizes and clear space

At 20 px and below, use the small drawing: a heavier line and far side, so nothing falls under a pixel. Keep clear space of one spot width (18 units) on every side; nothing else enters it.

Minimums

Mark alone: 16 px (small drawing). Horizontal lockup: 96 px wide. Stacked lockup: 64 px wide. App icon: every size iOS asks for, from the 1024 master.

Clear space

One spot width (18 units at the mark's scale) round the bounding box. In a lockup, the gap between mark and word is a quarter of the mark's width.

Lockups

The word is Nunito 900, CenterSpot in one word with two capitals. The mark sits on the word's middle, not its baseline.

Misuse

The tilt is the idea; keep it, and keep everything else plain.

App icon

The mark at 72% of the tile, centred on its optical middle, on the app's background. The 1024 master is a full square; iOS draws the rounded corners. A lime variant is kept for a light home screen test.

5

Motion

One curve, six durations. Fast out of the gate, a long gentle settle. Playback motion is the Timeline, not these; Reduce Motion swaps UI motion for crossfades and never touches an exported video.

Tokens.Motion.ease(_:)

.timingCurve(0.2, 0, 0, 1, duration:). Hover or focus a card below to play its duration.

Reduce Motion

Playback steps through with 0.3 s crossfades; the camera holds one still framing of the whole move; the chip pulse is off.

select 150 ms

Selecting a token or tile

step 220 ms

Going to a step; the chip pulse

sheet 320 ms

Sheets; welcome page turns

toast 200 ms

Toast in and out (stays 4 s)

camera 480 ms

The Broadcast camera's tilt

crossfade 300 ms

Reduce Motion's replacement

6

Icons

CenterSpot's own set (this lifts the D-51 hold), one icon for each SF Symbol the app uses, plus a few it needs. Drawn for football: the formation is a pitch, export is the 9:16 clip, tips are a whistle.

Grid

24-unit square, 2-unit padding (live area 20). One 2-unit stroke, round caps and joins, currentColor. Fills only where meaning needs one: our side is filled, play is solid. Keylines: circle 10, square 18, tall rectangle 14 × 20.

Moves

How a scenario plays out. The script is the same for every shape; who plays each part comes from the shape.

StepsEvery scenario is a move of 3 to 5 steps: the set-up, then one step after each beat (passes, runs, dribbles, a shot); the last step is the end.
Who movesThe side on the ball follows a short script by role, never by shirt number (“the left-most centre-back”), so a 3-5-2 plays it with its wing-back where a 4-3-3 uses its full-back.
The other sideReacts by rule after every beat: the nearest player presses, one tracks each runner, the keeper sets on the ball's side, the rest slide across and up with the ball. At set pieces they hold their zones. A beaten defender stays beaten.
How it endsAttacking: in a goal, except 3v1 keep the ball, which ends “Kept it, into space”. Defending: your team wins the ball, “Ball won”.
DefendingThe opponent runs the same move down the board and your team reacts, but nobody on your side is beaten and their last action (the shot) never comes. Instead your nearest outfield player wins the ball: a press arrow onto it and the note “10 wins the ball”. The keeper takes it only at a penalty or when there is no outfield defender (1v1 with the keeper): “1 saves it”. Their notes start “Their”; the last step is “Ball won”.
NotesEach step's note says what happens next, in shirt numbers, in the coach's handwriting: “11 plays inside to 10; 3 overlaps”.
ArrowsShown on the step they start from, fading as the players move. Run, pass or shot, dribble (below); a press only for the player who wins the ball on Defending.
